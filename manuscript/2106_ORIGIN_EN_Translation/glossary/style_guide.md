# Translation Style Guide

## Tone
- ORIGIN: Cold, precise, non-personified. Like a god, not a villain.
- Lu Xingyu: Restrained, rational, emotionally blocked. Short sentences.
- Aiden: Clinical, convinced, never cartoonishly evil.
- Narrator: Invisible. No authorial commentary. Show, don't tell.

## Sentence Structure
- Chinese favors short, paratactic sentences. Preserve this in English.
- Avoid: "Having completed his calculations, Lu Xingyu proceeded to..."
- Prefer: "He finished the numbers. Then he moved."

## Numbers
- Keep precise numbers (0.00017%) for ORIGIN's outputs
- Use approximate numbers ("a third", "nearly half") for human perception
- Never round ORIGIN's probabilities

## Dialogue Tags
- Minimize "he said / she said"
- Use action beats: "Lu Xingyu set down the cup. 'It's done.'"
- ORIGIN has no dialogue tags — its text appears in italics or block quotes

## Cultural Notes
- Family relationships: Don't explain Chinese family dynamics. Show through action.
- "Face", "guanxi": Translate contextually, not literally.
- Academic hierarchy: Chinese research institute structure — keep simple.

## Prohibited
- No footnotes in dialogue
- No translator's notes in body text
- No italicized foreign words except ORIGIN's outputs
- No explanatory parentheses in narration
